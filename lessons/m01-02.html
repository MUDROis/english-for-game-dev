State Verbs and the Rhythm of a Team

Today we describe people and teams through what they know, prefer and believe — and place the little words always, rarely and hardly ever exactly where native speakers put them. Lesson goal: talk about your teammates' states and habits without dragging state verbs into the Continuous.

Reading — Work Culture Diary

8 mintext + verb spotting

Four Time Zones, One Rhythm

Remote work suits small studios, but it demands discipline. Priya knows this better than anyone: she leads the design team from Lisbon while the artists sit in Helsinki and Krakow. She prefers async updates to long calls, and she believes a good written message saves an hour of everyone's time. "I understand the risk of loneliness in remote teams," she says, "so we need rituals, not surveillance."

Tom, the QA lead, owns two monitors and one strict rule: he always double-checks a bug report before he files it. He rarely works after six, and he never skips the Friday demo. The team respects his honesty: if the build is unstable, he says so — politely, but immediately. Meanwhile, the studio is hiring two new producers this month, and everybody is interviewing candidates between sprints. The rhythm holds because each person understands their part of it.

4

Grammar — State Verbs + Adverbs of Frequency

15 minrules + 4 exercises

STATE VERBS — no -ing, ever

  • Thinking: know, believe, understand, remember, mean, forget
  • Ownership & belonging: own, belong, contain, cost, fit
  • Preferences & feelings: prefer, want, need, like, love, hate
  • Being & seeming: be, seem, look like, appear, sound
I know the deadline — NOT ~I am knowing. This tool belongs to the studio — NOT ~is belonging.

ADVERBS OF FREQUENCY — position matters

  • Before the main verb: I always check the reports. · She hardly ever misses a deadline.
  • After am/is/are: He is never late for the stand-up.
  • Scale: always → usually → often → sometimes → rarely / seldom → hardly ever → never
⚠️ usually ≠ used to. And watch the traps: have a meeting (action — OK in Continuous), think about = consider (action), think that = opinion (state).

⚙️ Mini-table — action or state?

SentenceWhy?
I know the roadmap by heart.State — thinking verb, Present Simple only.
I am thinking about a career switch.Action (think about = consider) — Continuous is fine.
We have a stand-up every day.Have = organise (action) — Simple for routine.
We are having technical issues right now.Have = experience (action in progress) — Continuous.
She is never late.Frequency adverb after "be".
He always tests before lunch.Frequency adverb before the main verb.
🩹 Typical mistakes of Russian-speaking team leads: I am agree → I agree · She is knowing the build → She knows the build · I go always to the gym → I always go to the gym · He is late never → He is never late.

Grammar → Speaking — the main section of the lesson

10 mingoal: describe people through states and habits
The 5-second rule: if you catch yourself saying "I am knowing" — stop, restart the sentence calmly: "I know…". States never wear -ing.

🎙 Round 1 — Team Snapshot states + habits

Describe three people from your real team (or from the picture): what each of them knows, prefers, owns, believes and what they always / hardly ever / never do. 90 seconds.

Our art director prefers… She knows everything about… Our QA lead always double-checks… He hardly ever…

🎙 Round 2 — True or False About Me frequency adverbs

Say five sentences about yourself with always, usually, often, hardly ever, never — some true, some false. The teacher (or a colleague) guesses which are false. Example: I hardly ever play the games we test.

I usually start my day with… I hardly ever… I never… I always… before lunch.

🩹 Round 3 — Correction Sprint

The teacher says five sentences with typical mistakes (or you read them from the cards below). Correct each one out loud and explain why. Click a card to see the fix.

⚡ Speed Drill — a frequency adverb every 5 seconds

Open a card and instantly build a true sentence about your work or life with that adverb. Watch the position: before the main verb, after "be".

Writing — my role in the company

5 min50–90 words
Model answer — open it after your own version

I run a small game studio, and I know every part of our pipeline. My team works from home three days a week, and I always open the stand-up with one question: what is blocking you today? I prefer short meetings, and I never send messages after six. Our artists usually deliver on Fridays, and our QA lead double-checks every build. I hardly ever miss a deadline — because I respect the people who set it.

Homework

≤ 30 minutes in total
🧑‍🏫 Teacher notes

Goal: the student describes team members through states and habits, keeps state verbs out of the Continuous, and places frequency adverbs correctly.

Suggested flow: Warm-up: catch any I am agree / I am knowing from the previous lesson's error pocket → Listening: Priya and Tom — ask for states first, habits second → Reading: hunt state verbs in the text → Grammar: the mini-table "action or state?" is the heart of the lesson → Speaking: Round 3 (correction sprint) is where automation happens — do it aloud, fast.

Success criteria: Round 1 — three people described, zero state-verbs-in-Continuous; Round 3 — 5 corrections with reasons; speed drill — an instant answer for 6+ of 8 cards.

Error pocket: I am agree · she is knowing · adverb after the main verb (I go always) · adverb before "be" (he never is) · used to mixed up with usually.

Threads: the BBC future-of-work episode echoes the Warm-up; Priya and Tom return in Lessons 3 and 4; the hiring storyline prepares the articles of Lesson 3 (a producer, the producer we hired).
