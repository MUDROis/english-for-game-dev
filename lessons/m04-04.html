Pitch Your Next Quarter

Quarter finale: one two-and-a-half-minute pitch that proves all five futures live in your speech — and Module Test 4 to measure it. Lesson goal: present a plan that sounds decided (arrangements), prepared (going to), confident (will), scheduled (Future Perfect) and honest (might / probably / likely) — all in one story.

Reading — Tom's Toast

7 mina speech from the future

Tom's Toast — 19 December, the office party

Tom stands on a chair — the cats scatter. "A year ago I fixed bugs alone. Today I have a team, a test plan and a waiting list of volunteers who want to try our bug hunt day. Next year will be louder.

In January we will be hiring two more testers. By the summer, I think we will have doubled the QA team. Our new junior — that's you, don't hide — will probably write better bug reports than I did when I started. And our English student will definitely pass Module 4 — I have seen her flashcards.

So here is my forecast: next December we will be standing in a bigger office, the game will have won some award, and Pixel will still be sleeping on the router. To the future!"

Grammar Review — All Futures on One Board

8 minthe student explains first
How to use this section (from the plan): cover the table below with your hand, take a piece of paper, and draw the map yourself: five futures, one line each. Then compare with the table and only then do the exercises. Explaining it from memory is the exercise.

⚙️ The consolidation table

When you mean…Take…Example
A fixed arrangement: time, people, calendarPRESENT CONTINUOUSOn 12 December we are shipping the winter update.
An intention made earlier / visible evidenceGOING TOThe beta is clean, so the game is going to be ready early.
A decision now, a prediction, a promiseWILLGood question — I'll look into it today. The update will not break your saves.
A result by a deadlineFUTURE PERFECTBy March we will have shipped the expansion.
A process at a future momentFUTURE CONTINUOUSIn February we will be running the closed beta.
An honest forecastMIGHT / PROBABLY / LIKELYA hotfix might be necessary; we will probably find bugs first.

Speaking Challenge — My Next Quarter

15 min2.5 minutes + Q&A under pressure
The quota (from the plan): your pitch must contain 3 different future forms + 1 Future Perfect + 1 probability phrase. Structure: the plan → the dates → the risks → the win condition. Two takes: Take 1, self-review, then Take 2.

🎙 Take 1 — the pitch itself 2.5 minutes, no paper

Pitch your next quarter (work, QA course, English, or a personal project). Record it. Structure it like Dana: slide one — what is already fixed; slide two — results by dates; slide three — the plan in process; slide four — the win condition and the risks.

On (date) we are + -ing … By (month) we will have … In (month) we will be … A risk: (x) might …, but … is likely to …

🔍 Self-review — score your own Take 1 before repeating it

🎙 Take 2 — the pitch after the review better, not perfect

Same pitch, second attempt. Fix what you found in the self-review — keep everything that worked.

⚡ Q&A under pressure — five curveballs, no preparation

The teacher (or a friend) fires these at you after the pitch. Answer in full sentences with correct futures — this is the drill from the plan. Model answers open on click.

Homework

≤ 30 minutes in total
🧑‍🏫 Teacher notes

Goal: consolidate and measure the quarter: the student delivers a 2.5-minute pitch with the future quota, explains the choice of every form, then passes the 15-minute test without looking back at the rules.

Suggested flow: Warm-up: risk and win-condition questions; collect quota candidates → Listening: Dana's pitch; extract the slide structure (fixed → results → process → risks) → Reading: Tom's toast as the informal genre of the same skill → Grammar: the student draws the map BEFORE opening the table → Speaking: Take 1 → self-review → Take 2 → Test: one sitting, 15 minutes, note weak spots for Module 5.

Success criteria: the pitch — 3 forms + 1 Future Perfect + 1 probability phrase, no paper, ≤3 long pauses; self-review — 2 mistakes found by the student; the test — 70%+ overall.

After the test: Part 1–2 below 60% → Lessons 13–14; Part 3 → Lesson 15. The full error review happens at the start of Lesson 17.

Threads: the new partner from Lessons 13–14 finally sits at the table; Dana's answer to "what if the launch goes badly?" is the module's model of honest confidence; Tom's toast mirrors Priya's talk from Lesson 12 (same structure: past → now → forecast); Module 5 (Conditionals) will reopen the roadmap as "if the build fails" — keep Tom's hotfix answer in mind.
