Instructions: Polite but Precise

Lesson goal: give instructions that people actually follow. The imperative — Press, Hold, Do not click — is the shortest sentence in English, and the softeners — Please, Make sure you…, Be careful not to…, If in doubt, ask — make it human. An instruction plus a condition is the perfect pair: every checklist your team will ever read is built from them.

Reading — The Tester's Checklist

8 minimperatives + conditions

Harbour Lights Wiki · Page 19: First Week in QA — the checklist

Category: onboarding · Owner: Tom · Read time: 3 minutes

Welcome to the QA room. This checklist keeps your first week calm. Print it, tick it, follow it.

1. Open the build from the launcher — never from the folder. The launcher picks the right version.

2. Log in as a new user. Please make sure you test what a new player sees first.

3. Do not touch the live database. Test data lives in the sandbox; the live data is sacred.

4. If the screen freezes, take a screenshot and stop. Do not guess what the button "should" do.

5. Save every log before you close the ticket. Be careful not to delete the test files — the demo build lives in the same folder.

6. If in doubt, ask. A five-minute question saves a five-hour rewrite — you have read this line before, because it is also a law.

Grammar — Imperatives in Instructions

10 minrules + 4 exercises

IMPERATIVE — the verb is the sentence

  • Form: infinitive without to, no subject: Press Start. Hold the button. Restart the client.
  • Negative: Do not click / Don't touch the live database.
  • Polite tag: Press Start, please. — please at the end or the beginning.
  • Emphatic: Do check the logs. — do adds force, not future.
One instruction = one action. Six short imperatives beat one long sentence with "and then after that".

🪄 The softeners — same order, human voice

  • Please + imperative: Please save your work.
  • Make sure (you) + verb: Make sure you save every log.
  • Be careful not to + verb: Be careful not to delete the test files.
  • If in doubt, ask — the condition softens the order: If the screen freezes, take a screenshot and stop.
Instruction + condition = the perfect pair for regulations. You built the condition side in Lessons 17–18; now attach the verb.

Grammar → Speaking — the main section of the lesson

15 mingoal: instructions people follow
The 5-second rule: before giving an instruction ask: who is listening, and what happens if they don't understand? Blunt is for fires, polite is for people, precise is for checklists. And never "do not to…" — do not + verb.

🎙 Round 1 — QA Instructions 6 steps

Dictate to a beginner (the teacher, or your recorder) 6 steps of testing a build — imperatives plus at least 2 conditions. Model: Open the build, log in as a new user; if the screen freezes, take a screenshot and stop. 90 seconds.

Open the build. Log in as… Do not touch… If the screen freezes, take a screenshot. If in doubt, ask…

🎙 Round 2 — Onboarding Speech 60 seconds

Welcome a new member to your team (real or imagined) in a 60-second speech: what to do, what not to do, who to ask. Use at least 3 imperatives, 1 negative imperative and 1 "if in doubt" line.

Welcome! Start with… Don't worry about… If in doubt, come to me.

🎙 Round 3 — Politeness Slider (the plan's core drill)

Take each instruction below and say it three ways: blunt (fire alarm), neutral (colleague), maximally polite (partner or new junior). Then record your favourite trio. Same words, three temperatures.

Save the logs! Please save the logs. Please make sure you save the logs…

Homework

≤ 30 minutes in total
🧑‍🏫 Teacher notes

Goal: the student dictates instructions that are short, precise and adjustable in politeness — bare imperative for speed, softeners for people, conditions for safety — and stops wrapping instructions in "you should".

Suggested flow: Warm-up: processes and screen-time rules; collect real imperatives → Listening: onboarding day; count the instructions and their conditions → Reading: the wiki checklist mirrors the audio; Game Developer turns articles into instructions → Grammar: the three-temperatures table is the heart — the same order in three voices → Speaking: Round 3 (politeness slider) is the plan's core drill; Round 1 feeds the student's real QA portfolio → Writing: the checklist becomes the module's second artefact.

Success criteria: Round 1 — 6 steps, ≥2 conditions, no "you should"; Round 2 — 60 seconds, all three parts present; Round 3 — three temperatures without changing the meaning.

Error pocket: do not to + verb · "you press" instead of "press" · negative without do not · let's for instructions to one person · please overload (please twice in one sentence cancels out).

Threads: the wiki checklist quotes the "five-minute question" law from Lesson 17 — instructions grow out of laws; the onboarding junior returns in Lesson 20's scenario game as the newest team member; the checklist the student writes today is rehearsal for the Module 5 test's oral block ("continue the rule of the game").
