Grammar — Modal Passives & the Future Passive

10 minmodal + be + V3 + 4 exercises

MODAL PASSIVE — modal + be + V3

  • must be + V3 — the law: Every asset must be checked.
  • can be + V3 — possible / allowed: Decisions can be appealed. Bugs can be fixed overnight.
  • can't be + V3 — forbidden or impossible: Private code can't be uploaded.
  • should be + V3 — the nudge: Feedback should be answered within a week.
  • have to be + V3 — the external law: Builds have to be signed — the platform requires it.
The formula never changes: modal → be → V3. The Module 7 ladder rides on top of the Module 8 engine.

⏭ The future passive — will be + V3

  • The game will be released in May. — will release means you do it yourself.
  • Feedback will be collected every Friday. — the schedule is the news.
  • More testers will be hired next quarter. — HR reports love this form.
  • Questions keep the order: Will the game be released in May? — will the game release? is the active, a different claim.
  • One modal per verb: will must be does not exist. Choose the boss.
Careful — the Module 7 echo: the build must be checked (a rule) vs the build must have been checked (a deduction: somebody surely did it). Be + V3 is a law; have been + V3 is a guess.

⚙️ Mini-table — the rule, rebuilt as a modal passive

Active rule — who does itModal passive — what must happen
Two people must check every asset.Every asset must be checked by two people.
We will release the game in May.The game will be released in May.
You can't upload private code.Private code can't be uploaded.
The team should answer feedback quickly.Feedback should be answered quickly.
Dana reviews the deck every quarter.The deck is reviewed every quarter. (no modal? plain passive)
🩹 Typical mistakes of Russian-speaking policy writers: The build must checked → The build must be checked (modal + be + V3, the be is not optional) · The game will released in May → The game will be released in May · Rules must followed → Rules must be followed · The build must be check → must be checked (V3, not V1) · will must be approved → must be approved / will be approved (one modal per verb).

Grammar → Speaking — the main section of the lesson

15 mingoal: rules that sound like laws
The 5-second rule: before every rule ask silently: which modal, and is my be + V3 intact? If you catch yourself saying "assets must checked" — stop, the be ran away: "assets must be checked." A law without its be is not a law.

🎙 Round 1 — Your Policy Deck 6 cards

Write your studio's laws out loud — 6 policy cards in 90 seconds, every one a modal passive: assets must be tested before merge; feedback will be collected weekly; assets can be reused if they are open-source; private keys can't be shared. At least one card must be about the cats — they are management.

… must be checked / approved … can be reused / appealed … can't be uploaded / shared … will be reviewed / collected

🎙 Round 2 — The Future of the Studio roadmap in passive

Describe your studio's next year — 90 seconds, every point a future passive: the demo will be shown at the showcase; two testers will be hired; the sequel will be announced — eventually. Dreams are allowed, but they must be phrased properly.

The demo will be shown … More testers will be hired … The trailer will be released … … will be decided by the cats

🎙 Round 3 — Devil's Advocate defend the deck

The teacher attacks 3 of your policy cards ("but what if the deadline…?"). Defend them without leaving the passive: the deadline can be moved, but the checks can't be skipped. If you say an active rule — the point goes to the devil.

The deadline can be moved … The checks can't be skipped … Exceptions must be approved … Nothing will be changed without …

⚡ Speed Drill — sort the laws

Open a card and instantly name the modal and its strength — law, possibility, ban, nudge or schedule — then say the whole passive sentence aloud.

Homework

≤ 30 minutes in total
🧑‍🏫 Teacher notes

Goal: the student writes and speaks rules as modal passives — must/can/should/have to + be + V3, plus will be + V3 for the future — and never drops the be again. The Module 7 ladder (obligation → permission → advice) now lives inside the passive engine.

Suggested flow: Warm-up: AI-ethics questions that force should-be-imposed and must-be-approved → Listening: the policy deck; second pass copies one must-be card and one will-be card word for word → Reading: the wiki deck labels six cards by modal strength; the Guardian extension rebuilds one real passive with a modal → Grammar: the formula "modal → be → V3" is the whole lesson; the must-be vs must-have-been echo is the trap → Speaking: Round 1 is the fluency drill (6 cards), Round 2 moves to the future, Round 3 is the pressure test — defend the deck without leaving the passive → Writing: the rules page is pinned next to the release process from Lesson 29.

Success criteria: Round 1 — 6 cards, three different modals, every be present; Round 2 — 5 future passives, no will release slips; Round 3 — three defences, max one active escape; the rules page — 5 sentences, each a correct modal passive.

Error pocket: dropped be (must checked) · V1 for V3 (must be check) · double modals (will must be) · future active for passive (the game will release in May) · must be vs must have been (rule vs deduction).

Threads: the policy deck returns in the Lesson 32 release report (the patch must be approved); the devil's-advocate defence style reappears in Module 9 (reported speech: the rules were attacked — and defended); card six's sofa clause stays canon forever.
