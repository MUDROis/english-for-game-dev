How Long Have You Been Testing?

One bug, three weeks, one very patient QA lead. Lesson goal: talk about long processes that started in the past and are still running — have been + -ing — and keep them apart from finished, counted results (have + V3): how long vs how many.

Reading — Tom's Bug Diary

8 mintext + form spotting

Tom's Bug Diary

Day 1. I have been staring at this stack trace for two hours. The crash has appeared twice today, both times during autosave. I have reproduced nothing. Yet.

Day 4. I have been testing builds all week. So far I have collected nine crash logs; the ninth one finally says something useful. Day 8. I have been dreaming about this bug. Literally. Day 11. The developers have been rewriting the save system since Monday. I have been re-running my oldest test cases after every build. Day 15. We have fixed it. I have not deleted my notebook.

QA wisdom for newcomers: an intermittent bug is a pet, not an enemy. You do not defeat it in one fight; you have been building a relationship with it for weeks. Feed it logs. Watch its habits. Sooner or later, it comes home.

4

Grammar — Present Perfect Continuous

15 minrules + 4 exercises

PERFECT SIMPLE — the result and the numbers

  • have/has + V3. How much is done: I have tested nine builds.
  • A finished action with a visible product: We have fixed the bug.
  • Markers: already, just, so far, this month, ever, never
The question it answers: How many / How much?

PERFECT CONTINUOUS — the process still running

  • have/has been + -ing. Duration up to now: I have been testing since morning.
  • Recent temporary activity with visible traces: You have been crying! The screen has been flashing all day.
  • Markers: for three weeks, since October, all day, lately, how long
The question it answers: How long? — and the answer is "…and I still am."

⚙️ Mini-table — how many, or how long?

SentenceWhy?
I have collected nine crash logs.A counted result — Perfect Simple.
I have been collecting logs all week.A process in progress — Perfect Continuous.
She has written thirty bug reports this month.A number on the board — Simple.
She has been writing bug reports since September.An ongoing habit with a start point — Continuous.
How long have you known this engine?State verbs (know, believe, own) take the Simple form.
The fix has been in testing since Monday.Be is a state verb — Simple, even with since.
🩹 Typical mistakes of Russian-speaking team leads: I work here since 2021 → I have worked / have been working here since 2021 · How long do you know her? → How long have you known her? · I have been knowing him for years → I have known him for years · since three years → for three years · The game crashes since morning → The game has been crashing since morning.

Grammar → Speaking — the main section of the lesson

10 mingoal: how long, not how many
The 5-second rule: if you catch yourself saying "I work on this project since March" — stop, rebuild calmly: "I have been working on this project since March." Since/for + still running = Perfect Continuous.

Homework

≤ 30 minutes in total
🧑‍🏫 Teacher notes

Goal: the student produces have been + -ing + since/for automatically for still-running processes and keeps it apart from the counted Perfect Simple; for vs since is automatic.

Suggested flow: Warm-up: "how long…?" questions; demand the "still doing it" check → Listening: Tom's bug story; first the timeline, then the process/result split → Reading: mark every Perfect form in the diary P-Simple or P-Continuous → Grammar: the how-many/how-long mini-table is the heart → Speaking: Round 1 is the module's key drill; Round 2 trains real interview answers.

Success criteria: Round 1 — 3 × been + -ing with since/for; Round 2 — five natural answers, no "I work here since…"; Round 3 — 5 corrections with reasons.

Error pocket: present + since (I work here since) · Continuous with state verbs (have been knowing) · since + period (since three years) · result verbs with duration (have been fixing 400 bugs).

Threads: Tom's notebook and the save-system fix return in the Module Test 3; the "how long" interview prepares the duration part of the Lesson 12 monologue; the running habit sets up Lesson 11 (the ski season is a long process too).
