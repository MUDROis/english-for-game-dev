Grammar — The Certainty Ladder

10 mindeduction now & then + 4 exercises

DEDUCTION — about now

  • must be — almost sure: The servers are silent — the build must be frozen.
  • might / may / could be — possible: The crash might be the new shader pack.
  • can't be — impossible: It can't be the UI — the UI logs nothing.
  • Careful: the logical opposite of must be is can't be. Mustn't be means "forbidden" — different machine.
Compare: You mustn't touch main on Friday (a rule, Lesson 25) vs That mustn't be the cause ✗ → That can't be the cause ✓.

🕵️ About the past — the detective's toolkit

  • must have V3 — almost sure about yesterday: Someone must have left a debug flag on.
  • might have V3 — possible: The pipeline might have merged the old branch.
  • can't have V3 — impossible: Priya can't have pushed it — she was in Figma.
  • Structure is rigid: must / might / can't + have + the third form — "must have left", never "must has left".
The Russian «должно быть, он оставил» = he must have left — the have is not "possess", it is the perfect marker.

🩹 Typical mistakes of Russian-speaking detectives: It mustn't be the UI (for "impossible") → It can't be the UI · He must has left the flag → He must have left the flag · She can't to be the author → She can't be the author · He must have leave it → He must have left it (third form!) · It can't be — maybe (can't be = impossible, not "unlikely"; for "unlikely" use might not be).

Grammar → Speaking — the main section of the lesson

15 mingoal: guesses that sound professional
The 5-second rule: before every guess rate your certainty out loud: almost sure → must; possible → might; impossible → can't. If you catch yourself saying "it mustn't be the UI" about evidence — stop, rebuild: "it can't be the UI." Mustn't forbids, can't concludes.

🎙 Round 1 — Bug Detective 5 strange bugs

Five bizarre bug reports arrive. Reason out loud about each — what must have happened, what can't have happened, what might have happened? Model: the players can't have hacked the server; someone must have left a debug flag on. 2 minutes.

The coin counter shows -400 — someone must have … The menus speak French on one device only — it might be … The build passed on every machine except Tom's — it can't be … The save file weighs 4 GB — somebody must have …

🎙 Round 2 — Cat CSI 3 crimes, 3 alibis

Three household "crimes" with evidence. Name the culprit and the motive with the right certainty: the sofa is torn — it must have been the youngest; he might have been bored; it can't have been Pixel — he sleeps twenty hours a day.

The glass is on the floor, the cat looks proud — she must have … The keyboard has paw prints — it might have been … The fish bowl is empty, Pixel is asleep on it — it can't have been …

🎙 Round 3 — Lore Theorist

Build one fan theory about a universe you know (Marvel, a game, a series) — 90 seconds, all three levels: Loki can't have died — he must have left a variant behind; the next season might reveal it. Confidence is grammar, not volume.

He can't have died — he must have … The studio must have planned it since season one … The final scene might mean …

Homework

≤ 30 minutes in total
🧑‍🏫 Teacher notes

Goal: the student reasons with graded certainty — must be / might be / can't be about now, must have / might have / can't have about the past — and never uses mustn't for "impossible". This is the language of bug triage meetings and of every good fan theory.

Suggested flow: Warm-up: the three cat questions — every answer is already a deduction, polish the form → Listening: the red build; second pass copies one must-have and one can't-have sentence → Reading: the Detective Files rate six lines by certainty; the National Geographic extension applies the ladder to animals → Grammar: the mini-table's "one red build" is the exam shape; the EIAM video locks the ladder in one minute → Speaking: Round 1 (bug detective) is the work tool; Round 2 (cat CSI) is the fun rehearsal; Round 3 (lore theorist) stretches fluency → Writing: the incident report closes the case with all six forms.

Success criteria: Round 1 — all three past-deduction forms used correctly, no must-has-left slips; Round 2 — every verdict names its evidence; the report — 6 sentences covering must have, might have, can't have, must be, can't be.

Error pocket: mustn't be for "impossible" · must has V3 · can't to be · must have + first form (must have leave) · can't be used for "unlikely" (that is might not be) · spelling out "have" as /hæv/ instead of the weak /əv/ in speech.

Threads: the debug flag returns in Lesson 29 as reported speech ("Tom said someone must have left a flag on"); cat CSI is now a recurring segment; the certainty ladder reappears in Module 10 for game reviews ("this can't be the same studio that shipped the demo").
