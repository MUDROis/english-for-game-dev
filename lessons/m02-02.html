By the Time We Shipped… Past Perfect

Every failed deadline has a history — and English has a tense for that history. Lesson goal: explain what had happened (and how long it had been happening) before the moment everything went wrong — the language of post-mortems, delays and honest retrospectives.

Reading — Post-mortem: Project Snowdrift

8 mintext + tense spotting

Post-mortem: Project Snowdrift (internal document, abridged)

By the time we cancelled the open-world mode, we had spent eight months and forty per cent of the budget on it. Nobody had questioned the scope in the first three months, because everyone had assumed someone else was doing it. Our producer had warned us about the timeline as early as January, but her warnings had drowned in optimism.

The turning point came when the publisher played the build. By then, the testers had been filing the same navigation bugs for six weeks, and the design team had been redesigning the map every sprint. The lesson we wrote on the wall: a milestone that everybody is afraid to talk about is not a milestone — it is a debt. Since that project, we have cut scope early, and we have never regretted it. The game that shipped was half the size — and twice the game.

Grammar — Past Perfect (+ Continuous)

15 minrules + 4 exercises

PAST PERFECT — had done: the earlier past

  • An action completed before another past moment: By March we had finished only half the levels.
  • The "basement" of your story: The publisher was angry because she had expected a playable prototype.
  • Markers: by the time, before, after, when, already, never … before
Formula: past moment + what had (already) happened before it.

PAST PERFECT CONTINUOUS — had been doing: duration

  • How long up to a past point: We had been building the open world for eight months when we cut it.
  • Ongoing processes with visible results: Testers had been filing the same bugs for six weeks.
  • Markers: for / since / all day / how long + a past moment
State verbs stay simple: had known, had seemed — never ~had been knowing.

⚙️ Mini-table — which floor of the past?

SentenceWhy?
By the time we shipped, we had spent the whole budget.Before the past moment "shipped" → Past Perfect.
We had been testing for six hours when the crash appeared.Duration up to a past point → Past Perfect Continuous.
She had warned us in January, but we didn't listen.Earlier past (Perfect) + main event (Simple) — a classic pair.
Nobody had questioned the scope.Something had not happened before that moment.
By the time players saw the game, every level had been polished twice.Passive of the Past Perfect — the earlier past action on the object.
We cut the open world in March.The main event itself — Past Simple, no "had".
🩹 Typical mistakes of Russian-speaking team leads: By Friday we fixed only half the bugs (meaning: before Friday) → By Friday we had fixed only half the bugs · We were testing for six hours when the crash appeared → We had been testing for six hours when the crash appeared · After we shipped, we had celebrated → After we shipped, we celebrated (chronological order needs no Perfect).

Grammar → Speaking — the main section of the lesson

10 mingoal: the earlier past on demand
The 5-second rule: before every by the time / before / when, ask yourself: "did this happen before that moment?" If yes — had done. If it IS that moment — Past Simple.

🎙 Round 1 — Post-mortem lite by the time + had done

Analyse a deadline that slipped (real or from the listening). Requirement: at least 3 sentences with had done and 1 with had been doing. Model from the plan: By Friday we had fixed only half the bugs because we had been prioritising the wrong ones. 90 seconds.

By the time we noticed, we had… We had been prioritising… for weeks. Nobody had questioned… In the end we decided to…

🎙 Round 2 — Two Timelines connect two events

Take two milestones from your own life — for example "my QA course started" and "I found my first critical bug", or "we got funding" and "the prototype was ready". Connect each pair with had done / had been doing in 2–3 sentences. Do it for three pairs of milestones.

By the time I found my first critical bug, I had… I had been studying for … weeks when… The prototype was ready only after we had…

Writing — a mini post-mortem

5 min50–90 words
Model answer — open it after your own version

Project "Fog" was our most polite disaster. By the time we admitted the delay, we had missed two milestones and burned a third of the budget. Nobody had questioned the scope for months because everyone had assumed the others were on it. We had been polishing the menu screens while the core loop was still broken. In the end we cut the co-op mode, and the project finally shipped. The post-mortem had one line in bold: bad news does not improve with age.

Homework

≤ 30 minutes in total
🧑‍🏫 Teacher notes

Goal: the student reaches for the Past Perfect automatically after by the time / before / when and distinguishes had done (result) from had been doing (duration) — the core language of post-mortems.

Suggested flow: Warm-up: mark the "two floors of the past" in her answers → Listening: the delay story — build the timeline on paper before the detail questions → Reading: find all five Perfect forms in the post-mortem → Grammar: the mini-table "which floor of the past?" is the heart → Speaking: Round 2 (Two Timelines) personalises the tense — use her QA course milestones.

Success criteria: Round 1 — 3 × had done + 1 × had been doing; Round 3 — 5 corrections with reasons; the mini post-mortem contains ≥2 Past Perfect forms used correctly.

Error pocket: Simple after by the time · Perfect for main events in chronological order (after we shipped, we celebrated) · had been knowing · Past Continuous instead of Perfect Continuous with for/since + a past point.

Threads: Project Snowdrift echoes the listening story; the QA course storyline (Round 2) continues in Lesson 7; the post-mortem genre returns in Lesson 8 as the football retro.
