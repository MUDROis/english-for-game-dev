A Small Studio With a Big Month

The games industry is a hard market for small teams, but Harbour Lights is having a good month. The studio signed a contract with a German publisher — the publisher found them at a conference in autumn. A demo of the new game went out on a Friday; by Monday, the inbox was full of offers. "An investor called us too," Dana laughs, "but we don't need money — we need time."

The team is now finishing the winter update. The pipeline looks like this: the artists are drawing the cat café level, the QA team is testing every build, and business tasks wait until the evening. Dana still answers support tickets herself — she says feedback is the cheapest market research. Her advice for a first-time founder: love the process, keep the costs low, and remember that a prototype is worth more than a pitch.

4

Grammar — Articles: a / an / the / zero

15 minrules + 4 exercises

A / AN — one of many

  • First mention: I run a small studio. She had an idea.
  • Jobs: She is an art director.
  • One of a group: We need a working prototype.
a before a consonant sound: a studio, a university · an before a vowel sound: an idea, an hour, an MVP.

THE — we both know which one

  • Second mention: …had an idea. The idea was simple.
  • Unique in context: the studio, the market, the build we shipped yesterday
  • Superlatives and ordinals: the first version, the best publisher
There is only one, and both sides know it: the sun, the internet, the CEO.

⚙️ Zero article — when nothing is correct

UseExamples from your world
General ideas (uncountable)Business is hard. Money was short. Advice is cheap.
Plurals in generalInvestors take risks. Players demand quality. Deadlines matter.
Meals, languages, most countriesafter lunch, in Russian, in Finland (but the Netherlands, the UK)
Abstract process wordsGame design is hard work. Development takes time.
🩹 Typical mistakes of Russian-speaking founders: I am founder → I am a founder · The business is hard (general) → Business is hard · in the Finland → in Finland · a advice → a piece of advice (advice is uncountable).

Grammar → Speaking — the main section of the lesson

10 mingoal: a pitch with deliberate articles
The 5-second rule: not sure between a and the? Ask yourself: "does my listener already know which one I mean?" — yes → the, no → a. Then keep talking.

🎙 Round 1 — Elevator Pitch a / an → the

Your company in 60 seconds: what it is (a studio), what it makes (the studio makes…), and why it matters (zero article: quality matters). Record run 1, listen, then record run 2 — in run 2 the teacher checks only the articles.

I run a game studio… The studio makes… Our focus is… — no ads, no grind. We are looking for a publisher who…

🎙 Round 2 — Article Sprint a / the / zero

Eight short phrases about the industry with missing articles — insert a / an / the / zero out loud and explain each choice. Click the cards to check yourself.

📖 Round 3 — How I Started a → the chain

Tell the story of how you started your company (or a project) in 6 sentences. Rule: the first time you mention a thing — a / an; every time after — the. Example: I found an office. The office was cheap, but the heating was broken.

I started a company… The company had one client… We hired an artist… The artist became our art director…

⚡ Speed Drill — an article every 5 seconds

Open a card and instantly say the phrase with the correct article — then a full sentence about your business with it.

Homework

≤ 30 minutes in total
🧑‍🏫 Teacher notes

Goal: the student makes deliberate article choices in a pitch and a story: a/an for first mention, the for known context, zero for general ideas.

Suggested flow: Warm-up: collect the student's own article choices (right and wrong) on a virtual whiteboard → Listening: stop after the first two sentences and predict the articles → Reading: 5 noun phrases out loud → Grammar: the a→the chain is the core — drill it in Round 3 → Speaking: two pitch runs with a narrow focus in run 2.

Success criteria: Round 1 — run 2 has ≤2 article slips; Round 3 — a correct a→the chain for at least 4 objects; speed drill — instant answers on 6+ of 8 cards.

Error pocket: I am founder · the business is hard (general) · a advice / a information · in the Finland · silent articles before vowel sounds (an MVP).

Threads: the publisher storyline continues into Module 4 (plans); "how I started" recycles Lesson 2's state verbs; the cat café level from the reading returns in Lesson 4.
