By Q3 We Will Have Shipped

Deadlines deserve their own grammar. Lesson goal: describe the process at a future moment (Future Continuous — in March we will be stress-testing the servers) and the result by a deadline (Future Perfect — by March we will have shipped the expansion) without mixing them up.

I will have closed three hundred bugs and written my two-hundredth test case — my own little milestone. The cats will have slept through every single meeting, as usual.

4

Grammar — Future Continuous & Future Perfect

15 minrules + 4 exercises

FUTURE CONTINUOUS — the process at a moment

  • will + be + V-ing: an action in the middle at a future moment: In December we will be stress-testing the servers.
  • A background plan around a time: This time next month I will be drinking cocoa at the office party.
  • Polite questions about plans: Will you be using the test lab on Friday?
The camera is inside the action: it started before the moment and continues after it.

FUTURE PERFECT — the result by a deadline

  • will + have + V3: done before a future moment: By March we will have shipped the expansion.
  • Countable results at a deadline: By Friday I will have written two hundred test cases.
  • Markers: by + date, by then, by the end of …
The camera is after the finish line, looking back: it is done.

⚙️ Mini-table — process or result?

SentenceWhy?
By the 12th we will have shipped everything.By + date = result by a deadline → Future Perfect.
In March we will be stress-testing the servers.An action in process in that month → Future Continuous.
This time next year we will be skiing.This time … = inside the moment → Future Continuous.
By the end of January Dana will have signed the plan.The signature will be done by then → Future Perfect.
At six o'clock I will still be writing the report.Still in the middle at that moment → Future Continuous.
By Sunday evening we will have tried the red slope.Done before Sunday ends → Future Perfect.
🩹 Typical mistakes of Russian-speaking team leads: By March we will ship the expansion (if you mean "done by then") → By March we will have shipped the expansion · By Friday we will be finishing the report → By Friday we will have finished the report · Tomorrow at six I will work → Tomorrow at six I will be working (in process) · we will have been shipping by March → we will have shipped by March (a result needs the simple Perfect).

Grammar → Speaking — the main section of the lesson

10 mingoal: deadlines with two cameras
The 5-second rule: hear by + date → build the result (will have + V3). Hear this time / at that moment → build the process (will be + V-ing). If you catch "by Friday we will be finishing" — stop, rebuild: "by Friday we will have finished."

🎙 Round 1 — Deadline Math result by the flag

Five deadlines are coming. For each, say one full sentence in Future Perfect about your real work or QA course. Model: The beta is on 14 March — by then we will have fixed the netcode. 90 seconds.

🎙 Round 2 — This Time Next Year process and result

Say 5 sentences about next year: alternate the two forms — one about life at the studio (or your studies), one about the ski season. Model: In January we will be hiring two testers; by June I will have passed two exams. 120 seconds.

Homework

≤ 30 minutes in total
🧑‍🏫 Teacher notes

Goal: the student separates the two "long-range" futures: Future Continuous = the process at a future moment, Future Perfect = the result by a deadline — and hears the difference between by + date and this time + moment.

Suggested flow: Warm-up: collect "in two years / by the end of this quarter" answers and mark process vs result → Listening: the three flags; second pass sorts will have done vs will be doing → Reading: Tom's future status update; label every new form → Grammar: the two cameras is the heart; the video consolidates the Continuous → Speaking: Round 1 is the module's key drill.

Success criteria: Round 1 — five clean Future Perfect sentences with real deadlines; Round 2 — a clean 50/50 alternation, no form mixing; Round 3 — 5 corrections with the "which camera?" reason.

Error pocket: by + date with the Continuous (will be finishing by Friday) · will have been shipping for a countable result · missing be in the Continuous (will working) · Russian calque "until March we will already release" → by March we will already have released.

Threads: the three flags continue the roadmap board from Lesson 13; Tom's joke update mirrors the "jargon memo" genre; the ski-club victory lap pays off the booking from Lesson 11; the roadmap Writing genre returns in Lesson 16 as the pitch slide deck.
