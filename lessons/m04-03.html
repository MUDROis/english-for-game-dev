Likely, Probably, Might — Forecasting

The industry runs on forecasts: market reports, launch predictions, tournament odds. Lesson goal: grade your confidence from might (30%) to definitely (95%) and sound like an honest analyst — sure enough to be trusted, careful enough to be right.

Reading — Esports at the Olympics?

8 minblog post + certainty grading

From the studio blog: Esports at the Olympics — a forecast

Will esports join the Olympics? A year ago, my answer was a confident no. Today my answer is: probably, but not soon.

The Olympic committee has already changed its mind about several "new" sports, and younger viewers are likely to push the organisation further. Money may do the rest: advertising in esports is growing, and the Olympic brand could use a younger audience.

But there are problems, and they might not go away. Game publishers own their games, and they may not want to share control with a committee. Cheating is another risk — one big scandal could kill the idea for ten years.

So here is my forecast for 2032: esports will probably be part of the festival in some form — as a demonstration, not a medal sport. A full Olympic medal for a video game? It might happen one day, but don't buy tickets yet.

Grammar — The Confidence Ladder

15 minrules + 4 exercises

THE LADDER — from 30% to 95%

WordConfidencePatternExample
might≈ 30% — a real option, a real doubtmight + verbLag might walk across the keyboard again.
may≈ 50% — an open doormay + verbThere may be one hotfix in week one.
could≈ 50% — possible in theorycould + verbThe red slope could open next week.
probably≈ 70% — more yes than nowill probably + verbThe update will probably get a 90% score.
likely≈ 75% — the evidence says yesbe likely to + verbWe are likely to pass 60,000 wishlists.
definitely≈ 95% — as sure as work allowswill definitely + verbThe servers will definitely survive the launch.
Pattern watch: might / may / could attach to the verb directly; probably / definitely sit after will; likely needs its frame be likely to. Negative forecasts: might not, may not, probably won't, unlikely to.

⚙️ Mini-table — same forecast, three confidence levels

ForecastWhat it tells the listener
The game might win an award."I have heard rumours. Don't quote me."
The game could win an award."There is a real path to it — good reviews, good timing."
The game will probably win an award."Based on what I see — more yes than no."
The game is likely to win an award."The signs are strong; I stand behind this forecast."
The game will definitely win an award."Only for facts and promises — use rarely!"
🩹 Typical mistakes of Russian-speaking team leads: It will may rain → It may rain · We are probable to ship in Q2 → We are likely to ship in Q2 · Probably I will go (sounds hesitant-Russian word order) → I will probably go · The game will definitely be a hit (a guess!) → The game will probably be a hit.

Grammar → Speaking — the main section of the lesson

10 mingoal: forecasts with honest odds
The 5-second rule: before a forecast, choose your number first (30? 50? 70? 90?), then take the word from the ladder. If you catch yourself saying "it will may work" — stop: one confidence word per sentence is enough.

🎙 Round 1 — Forecast Bar the teacher guesses your %

Make 6 forecasts: two about the game industry, two about football or the World Cup, two about your own company or studies. Use a different confidence word in each — don't name the number. The teacher guesses your percentage. 120 seconds.

It might … It could … It will probably … … is likely to …

🎙 Round 2 — Uncertain Founder honest answers to an investor

Writing — a letter to yourself in one year

5 min60–100 words
Model answer — open it after your own version

Dear me in a year, here is my forecast. You will probably work as a junior QA engineer by then — your study plan is strong. You are likely to pass the mid-term exam in March if you keep the schedule. The English course will definitely be finished — no doubts there. Some goals may move to the next year: the console project and the skiing trip to the big resort. And you might finally learn to say no to extra tasks. I could be wrong about one thing: I hope it's about the salary. See you in twelve months.

Homework

≤ 30 minutes in total
🧑‍🏫 Teacher notes

Goal: the student grades uncertainty consciously: might (30%) → may/could (50%) → probably (70%) → likely (75%) → definitely (95%) — and uses the right frame for each word (might + verb, will probably, be likely to).

Suggested flow: Warm-up: collect forecasts and force a confidence label on each → Listening: the forecast board; second pass collects confidence words per speaker → Reading: the Olympics blog; grade every probability word 10–90% → Grammar: the ladder table is the heart; the video fixes the bottom half → Speaking: Round 2 is the module's key drill — honest uncertainty is a leadership skill.

Success criteria: Round 1 — 6 forecasts, 6 different confidence words, the teacher's guesses are within ±20%; Round 2 — 5 answers without fake certainty and without total fog; Round 3 — 5 corrections with the frame explained.

Error pocket: will may (two modals together) · probable to instead of likely to · probably before the subject (Russian calque) · definitely for guesses · may be vs maybe spelling.
