Reading — The Advice Desk replies

10 minletters + strength spotting

Harbour Lights Wiki · Page 20: Advice Desk — Selected Replies

Category: community · Owner: whoever is free at lunch · Status: replies are advice, not orders — mostly

Grammar check: every reply chooses a strength. must / have to = a law · mustn't / don't have to = forbidden / optional · should / ought to / had better = the ladder · must be / might be / can't be = deduction.

Letter 1 — the overwhelmed tester: "I test 14 hours a day and I am scared to say no." Reply: You must learn to say no — kindly, but today. You had better write down your hours and show them to your lead; burnout can't be fixed with heroics.

Letter 2 — the future QA student: "How do I get into game QA?" Reply: You should start with public bug trackers — real tickets, real practice. You don't have to know how to code yet.

Letter 3 — the scope-creep indie: "My 'small' game now has 60 hours of story." Reply: You ought to cut it in half — twice. You had better not add a single new feature before the demo ships.

Letter 4 — the scared skier: "First slope on Saturday. Terrified." Reply: You have to learn the snowplough stop before anything else — that is the law of the mountain. You mustn't race your friends on day one.

Letter 5 — the cat owner: "My cat screams at 5 a.m. every night." Reply: She might be hungry — but it can't be love, sadly. You need to move the last meal later. (Boss co-signed this one.)

⚙️ Mini-table — the five traps the test will hunt

TrapWrongRight
Optional ≠ forbiddenYou mustn't bring cake — it's optionalYou don't have to bring cake
must + bare infinitiveYou must to sign the NDAYou must sign the NDA
had better not (no to)You had better not to sign itYou had better not sign it
Impossible = can't beIt mustn't be the UIIt can't be the UI
Past deduction: have + V3He must has left / must have leaveHe must have left
🩹 One more classic: past obligation is had to — yesterday I must stay does not exist. And questions ask Do I have to…?, not Must I…? (rare, formal).

Grammar → Speaking — the Advice Desk (final)

15 mingoal: the right strength on demand
The 5-second rule: before every answer ask silently: law, nudge, alarm or guess? If you catch yourself shouting had-betters at everything — stop, downgrade: most problems are shoulds. Save the alarm for fires, the laws for laws.

🎙 Round 1 — The Advice Desk 5 problems, spontaneous

Five problems land on your desk — friends, colleagues, strangers. Answer each spontaneously, choosing the strength yourself — and name it: "that is a must", "that is only a should". The teacher may bring five more problems of their own. 3 minutes.

The overwhelmed tester → you must …, you had better … The future QA student → you should …, you don't have to … The scope-creep indie → you ought to …, you had better not … The silent cat at 5 a.m. → she might be …, it can't be …

🎙 Round 2 — Detective Wrap-Up one mystery, three levels

One last case: the studio's trophy has moved two metres to the left overnight. Solve it with all three deduction types — must have (your main theory), might have (the alternative), can't have (what you exclude). 90 seconds, evidence required.

It must have been …, because … It might have been …, if … It can't have been …, because …

🎙 Round 3 — Self-Check (from the plan)

Name 3 modal mistakes you personally made in this module — from exercises, recordings or the letters — and say the rule against each one in your own words. Then promise the fix: I said "mustn't be" for impossible → "can't be" — from today, can't concludes, mustn't forbids.

Homework

light — test week
🧑‍🏫 Teacher notes

Goal: consolidate Module 7 — the student switches between obligation, prohibition, advice, warning and deduction on demand, and measures the result honestly in Module Test 7 (5 + 5 written items and one recorded advice block).

Suggested flow: Warm-up: the skiing questions produce laws (must learn the snowplough), warnings (had better not race) and advice (should rest) — the whole module in one topic → Listening: the Advice Desk; second pass copies one had-better answer and one can't-be answer → Reading: five letters, five strengths — label each reply before checking → Grammar: the student fills the six-cell modal map from memory (the plan's consolidation task) before looking; the five traps table is the pre-test briefing → Speaking: Round 1 is the plan's final Advice Desk with named strengths; Round 2 closes the detective arc; Round 3 is the plan's self-check → Test: 10 written items + 1 recorded task → Homework: light by design.

Success criteria: Round 1 — five answers, at least three different strengths, each named aloud; Test 7 — 70%+ written with both parts attempted; the modal map — all six cells filled with the student's own examples.

Error pocket (the five the test hunts): mustn't for "optional" · must to · had better not to · mustn't be for "impossible" · must has left. Every slip found in Round 3 goes to the priority list with a promised fix.

Threads: the Advice Desk letters return in Module 9 as reported speech ("the tester said she had to learn to say no"); the modal map becomes the base of Lesson 30's modal passives (rules must be followed); the trophy case mystery stays open until the cats confess — or don't.
