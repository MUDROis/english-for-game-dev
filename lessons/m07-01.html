Must, Have To, or Optional? The Rules of the Month

Module 7 swaps dreams for duty — the language of a team lead. Lesson goal: talk about rules like a boss — what is truly required (must / have to / need to), what is forbidden (mustn't) and what is optional (don't have to) — and hear the difference between your own laws and everybody else's requirements.

Grammar — Must, Have To, Need To

10 minobligation + 4 exercises

OBLIGATION — whose law is it?

  • must — the speaker's own law: Testers must double-check on two devices. (I say so, and I mean it.)
  • have to — an external law: We have to log our hours — the publisher requires it. (Not my idea.)
  • need to — necessity: We need to fix the build before the demo. (The situation demands it.)
  • must has no past and no future: yesterday → had to, next month → will have to.
Questions are human: Do I have to attend? — "Must I attend?" is rare and very formal.

🚫 The trap — mustn't vs don't have to

  • mustn't = forbidden: You mustn't push to main on Fridays. (Do not do it!)
  • don't have to = optional: You don't have to come on Friday — it is a day off. (Nobody forces you.)
  • Same verb family, opposite meanings — Russian «нельзя» vs «не обязательно».
  • Both are followed by the bare infinitive: must push, have to push — no -to after must, no -ing.
Mega-test: your lead says "You don't have to test the tutorial again." — good news (free evening) or bad news (fireable offence)? Good news: it is optional, not forbidden.

⚙️ Mini-table — whose law is it?

SentenceWhy?
Testers must double-check on two devices.Tom's own law → must (the speaker's internal rule).
We have to fill in the time tracker.The publisher requires it → have to (external).
You mustn't push to main on Fridays.Forbidden → mustn't (prohibition).
Freelancers don't have to attend standups.Optional → don't have to (no obligation).
Yesterday we had to roll back the patch.Past obligation → had to (must has no past).
🩹 Typical mistakes of Russian-speaking rule-makers: She musts check the build → She must check the build (modals never take -s) · You must to sign the NDA → You must sign the NDA (bare infinitive) · We don't must work weekends → We mustn't / don't have to work weekends (decide: forbidden or optional?) · Yesterday I must stay late → Yesterday I had to stay late · You mustn't bring cake — it's optional → You don't have to bring cake (optional ≠ forbidden).

Grammar → Speaking — the main section of the lesson

15 mingoal: rules that sound like rules
The 5-second rule: before every rule ask silently: whose law is it — mine, theirs, or nobody's? If you catch yourself saying "you mustn't bring cake, it's optional" — stop, rebuild: "you don't have to bring cake." The modal is the message.

🎙 Round 1 — Founder's Code 8 rules

Your company's constitution, out loud — 8 rules in 90 seconds, mixing all four: testers must double-check on two devices; freelancers don't have to attend standups; you mustn't push to main on Fridays; everyone has to log hours. Two serious laws, two funny ones — but every one needs the right modal.

Testers must … Everyone has to … You mustn't … Interns don't have to …, but they need to …

🎙 Round 2 — Two Companies compare the worlds

Compare your (real or dream) studio with a giant imaginary corporation — 90 seconds. What does each world force you to do, and what does it leave free? In a big publisher you have to fill in weekly reports; in my studio we must only ship quality — and feed the cats.

In a big publisher you have to … In my studio we must … Big-company testers mustn't … Our freelancers don't have to …

Homework

≤ 30 minutes in total
🧑‍🏫 Teacher notes

Goal: the student states rules like a lead — must / have to / need to for obligation, mustn't for prohibition, don't have to for no obligation — and never confuses mustn't with don't have to again. Class opens with the Module Test 6 review: the multiverse conditionals get one last public polish.

Suggested flow: Warm-up: founder duties, hated-but-required tasks, one never-rule → Listening: the memo; second pass copies one must-rule and one don't-have-to-rule word for word → Reading: the wiki board labels six rules by source; the BBC Worklife extension rephrases three real-world obligation phrases → Grammar: the mini-table's "whose law?" rows decide everything; the Grammar Gameshow episode lands the trap → Speaking: Round 1 (Founder's Code) is the fluency drill — full sentences, mixed modals; Round 2 forces have to vs must side by side → Writing: the memo is the artefact Dana pins on the wall next class.

Success criteria: Round 1 — 8 rules, all four forms present, no bare-infinitive slips; Round 2 — at least 3 have-to vs must contrasts; the memo — 6 rules with at least one optional and one forbidden.

Error pocket: must + to (must to sign) · mustn't for "optional" (you mustn't bring cake) · don't must · -s on must (she musts) · past of must (yesterday I must stay → had to) · answering "Must I?" with "No, you mustn't" (the right no is "No, you don't have to").

Threads: the Rules of the Month return in Lesson 30 as modal passives (rules must be followed); mustn't vs don't have to will be hunted again in Module Test 7; the cats-as-management joke is now canon and will keep hijacking agendas through Module 8.
