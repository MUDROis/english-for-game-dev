Should, Ought To, Had Better The Mentor Mode

Lesson goal: give advice with exactly the right force — a soft should, a formal ought to, or a red-alert had better (and its negative had better not) — because a lead who shouts warnings at everything soon advises nobody.

Grammar — The Advice Ladder

10 minshould · ought to · had better + 4 exercises

ADVICE — the polite two

  • should — your opinion, the default: You should build a small demo first.
  • ought to — the same meaning, softer and a bit more formal: You ought to register the name early.
  • Negative: shouldn't — You shouldn't argue with players in comments. (ought not to exists but is rare)
  • Questions are human: Should I sign the offer?
Both are followed by the bare infinitive: should build, ought to build — no -ing, no to after should.

🚨 The warning — had better

  • had better = do it, or else: You had better back up the build — or one crash erases a month.
  • Negative: had better not (no to!): You had better not sign it unread.
  • It points at one specific situation, not general habits — and it is what a lead says when the risk is real.
  • The past form is a lie: "had" here is not past time — You had better fix it now means now.
Volume test: should = a nudge · ought to = a nudge in a suit · had better = the fire alarm.

⚙️ Mini-table — one problem, three volumes

ProblemThe advice ladder
A junior ships untested UIYou should run the smoke test first. → normal advice.
The same junior, politely, in writingYou ought to document which builds you tested. → formal, softer.
The release is in one hour and the build is redYou had better roll back now — every minute costs wishlists. → warning.
Someone argues with a player in the commentsYou had better not answer that — screenshots live forever. → warning, negative.
A new lead asks about 1-to-1 meetingsYou should hold them weekly; you ought to cancel only twice a year. → advice + a formal addition.
🩹 Typical mistakes of Russian-speaking mentors: You should to test it → You should test it (no to after should) · You had better to back up → You had better back up · You had better not to sign it → You had better not sign it · You shoulds ask → You should ask (modals never take -s) · Yesterday you should call me (about the past) → You should have called me (past advice — Module 8 will open this door wider).

Grammar → Speaking — the main section of the lesson

15 mingoal: the right advice at the right volume
The 5-second rule: before every piece of advice ask silently: how bad is it if they ignore me? A nudge → should. A formal nudge → ought to. A real risk → had better. If you catch yourself saying "you had better drink more water" — that is should. Save the alarm for fires.

🎙 Round 1 — Mentor Mode 5 tips

A fictional beginning indie developer asks you for help. Give 5 tips in 90 seconds — one should, one ought to, one had better, one shouldn't, one free. Model: you had better test on old phones first.

You should start with … You ought to register … You had better back up …, or … You shouldn't promise …

🎙 Round 2 — The Advice Ladder one situation, three volumes

Take one situation (a late build, an angry player, a crunching friend) and give the same core advice three times — soft should → formal ought to → red-alert had better. Feel the volume change; keep the grammar bare.

You should … (a nudge) You ought to … (in a suit) You had better …, or … (the fire alarm)

🎙 Round 3 — Exit Interview (roleplay)

The teacher plays a colleague leaving for a new job; you are the lead giving recommendations — 2 minutes: what should they keep doing, what ought they to change, what had they better never do again? (Self-study: play both roles and record both voices.)

You should keep … You ought to change … You had better never … again

Homework

≤ 30 minutes in total
🧑‍🏫 Teacher notes

Goal: the student gives advice at three volumes — should / ought to / had better (+ had better not) — and stops using had better for trivial preferences. The ladder from Lesson 25 (obligation) now grows a second rail (advice).

Suggested flow: Warm-up: advice to your past self, your best mentor, the AI-crime limit → Listening: the Mentor Hour; second pass copies one should and one had better word for word → Reading: the Mentor Board labels six lines by strength; the Game Developer extension converts two article statements into advice → Grammar: the mini-table's "one problem, three volumes" is the heart of the lesson; the Grammar Gameshow episode adds the laughs → Speaking: Round 2 (the ladder) is the module's control drill — the same advice three ways; Round 3 is the plan's exit-interview roleplay → Writing: the letter to Jonas mirrors the listening.

Success criteria: Round 1 — 5 tips, all four named forms present; Round 2 — three volumes of the same advice with no to after should/better; the letter — 6 sentences, 3 different advice constructions, at least one had better not.

Error pocket: should to · had better to · had better not to · shoulds · past advice with should + past (yesterday you should call → should have called) · had better for general habits (you had better brush your teeth — that is should).

Threads: Jonas's studio becomes the course's second location — his first launch lands in Lesson 29 (reported speech will quote his standups); the advice ladder returns in Lesson 28's Advice Desk; "should have called" opens officially in Module 8's modal review.
