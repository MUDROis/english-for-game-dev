Grammar — Present Perfect vs Past Simple

15 minrules + 4 exercises

PAST SIMPLE — the timeline with dates

  • Finished actions with a stated or obvious time: We launched our first game in 2023.
  • Events in sequence: I registered the company, rented a desk and started.
  • Markers: yesterday, last year, in 2023, two days ago, when
If the sentence says when — Simple wins. No discussion.

PRESENT PERFECT — the result on your desk now

  • have/has + V3. A result that matters now, time not stated: We have doubled the team.
  • Life experience up to now: Have you ever shipped on a Friday? I have never missed a payday.
  • Unfinished periods: so far, this year, since May, for two years
No exact dates inside the Perfect. “…have shipped it yesterday” breaks the form.

Grammar → Speaking — the main section of the lesson

10 mingoal: results without dates, events with them
The 5-second rule: if you catch yourself saying "I have finished it yesterday" — stop, rebuild calmly: "I finished it yesterday." A date kills the Perfect; so far kills the Simple.

Homework

≤ 30 minutes in total
🧑‍🏫 Teacher notes

Goal: the student separates the Present Perfect (result, experience, unfinished time — no dates) from the Past Simple (finished time with a date) and stops translating Russian perfectives word for word.

Suggested flow: Warm-up: collect achievements, ask "date or no date?" after every answer → Listening: the achievements wall; first pass for gist, second pass with the date filter → Reading: label every verb in Dana's post as pf or ps → Grammar: the mini-table is the heart; the video consolidates → Speaking: Round 1 is the module's key drill; keep the 5-second rule visible.

Success criteria: Round 1 — exactly 2 dated facts + 3 undated results, no tense pauses; Round 2 — all five answers with correct forms; Round 3 — 5 corrections with reasons.

Error pocket: Perfect + finished date (have shipped yesterday) · Simple with unfinished markers (since, so far, this year) · Did you ever…? for experience · been to vs gone to.

Threads: the achievements wall returns in Lesson 12 (the journey talk); Tom's 400 bugs open the bug-diary storyline of Lesson 10; the anniversary post is the model genre for the Writing section of every module.
