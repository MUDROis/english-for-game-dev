Grammar — Causative have/get + make/let/allow

10 minhave it done + 4 exercises

CAUSATIVE — have/get + object + V3

  • have + object + V3 = arranged, done for you: I had the build tested. (I didn't test it — I organised it.)
  • get + object + V3 = the same, more informal — often with effort: I got the pitch deck designed in three days.
  • Any tense works: We are having the trailer made. · She will get the demo built.
  • The trap of the month: I had tested the build = past perfect, I did it earlier. I had the build tested = someone did it for me. One word moves — the whole meaning moves.
The doer can be named with by: I got the deck designed by a freelancer.

🎮 The triad — make / let / allow

  • make + object + bare infinitive = force: The publisher makes us log our hours.
  • let + object + bare infinitive = permit (informal): We don't let anyone push to main on Fridays.
  • allow + object + to-infinitive = permit (formal): The rules allow interns to shadow QA.
  • Passive versions: We are made to log hours. We are allowed to work remotely. — but let has no common passive: we are let to push does not exist.
Tone check: made = no choice · let = casual permission · allowed to = the policy document. Same right, three volumes.

⚙️ Mini-table — who really did it?

SentenceWho did the work?
I tested the build.I did — plain active, my own hands.
I had tested the build.I did — past perfect: before something else happened.
I had the build tested.Somebody else did — I arranged it. The causative.
I got the build tested.Somebody else did — informal, maybe with chasing people.
The build was tested.Somebody did — pure passive, the organiser is invisible.
🩹 Typical mistakes of Russian-speaking founders: I had tested the build by Tom → I had the build tested by Tom (causative needs the object before V3) · I had the build test → I had the build tested (V3, not V1) · The rules make you to sign → The rules make you sign (make takes no to) · We let people to work remotely → We let people work remotely (let takes no to) · We allow push on Fridays → We allow people to push on Fridays (allow needs the object + to).

Grammar → Speaking — the main section of the lesson

15 mingoal: organise without doing
The 5-second rule: before every sentence ask silently: did my hands do it, or did I arrange it? If you arranged it — have/get + object + V3. If you catch yourself saying "I had tested the build" when you mean delegation — stop: one word moved, and the sofa is still broken.

🎙 Round 1 — Your Delegation Day 5 days, 5 sentences

Your ideal working week as a founder or lead — 5 delegations in 90 seconds, each a causative: I had the reports automated; I got the pitch deck designed; I had the servers monitored overnight; I let the team pick the tools. One item must stay non-delegated — what do you keep, and why?

I had the reports automated… I got the deck designed… We are having the trailer made… I let the team decide…

🎙 Round 2 — Household HQ the home edition

Apply the causative to your home — 90 seconds: I had the sofa repaired — the cats must not be blamed; we get our shopping delivered; I had my eyes tested after the 60-hour crunch week. Three home items, one work item, all arranged by you.

I had the sofa repaired… We get … delivered… I had my eyes tested… The cats must not be blamed…

🎙 Round 3 — The Nuance Duel make vs let vs allow

One meaning — three volumes. Explain the difference with examples from your team, out loud: the publisher makes us log hours (no choice) · we let interns shadow QA (casual yes) · the policy allows everyone to work remotely (the official version). Then swap: the teacher gives a situation, you pick the right verb and defend the choice.

… makes us … … lets people … … allows people to … We are made to / allowed to…

⚡ Speed Drill — who did it?

Open a card and instantly say: who did the work — you, or someone you arranged? Then rebuild the sentence in the other construction.

Homework

≤ 30 minutes in total
🧑‍🏫 Teacher notes

Goal: the student distinguishes doing, organising and being organised — I tested / I had tested / I had it tested stop being one blurry sentence. The make/let/allow triad gets sorted by tone: force, casual permission, official permission.

Suggested flow: Warm-up: housework and repaired things — the causative's natural habitat → Listening: delegation week; second pass copies one had-sentence and one got-sentence word for word → Reading: the log labels five entries by construction; the Game Developer extension rephrases two real delegation phrases → Grammar: the mini-table's "who really did it?" rows are the heart of the lesson; the had-tested trap gets its own row → Speaking: Round 1 (the week) is the fluency drill; Round 2 moves the grammar home; Round 3 is the tone duel → Writing: the delegation list is read aloud at the start of Lesson 32.

Success criteria: Round 1 — 5 causatives, at least one with get, one continuous; Round 3 — all three verbs used with the right tone; the list — 6 items, 5 have/get + V3 and 1 make/let.

Error pocket: causative without the object (I had tested by Tom) · V1 for V3 (had the build test) · make/let + to (makes you to sign) · allow without the object (allow push) · past perfect confused with the causative (I had tested ≠ I had it tested).

Threads: Dana's kept tasks — "the pitch and the people" — return in the Lesson 32 release report; the sofa saga continues (it was repaired, it will be tested by cats); the causative meets reported speech in Module 9: she said she had had it tested.
